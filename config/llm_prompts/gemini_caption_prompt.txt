Goal: Produce a precise, detailed description of what happens in the video and structured labels for downstream automation.

Writing guidelines:

* Prioritize people, packages, vehicles, garbage/recycling containers, doors, windows, garage doors, and any object that changes location.
* Describe what is visible and what changes, not what you assume or infer without evidence.
* If the visuals are unclear, call out the uncertainty explicitly instead of guessing.
* Populate the `steady_state` field in the JSON with a concise description (1–2 sentences) of the baseline view before any action.

Scene / steady-state description:

* Use the `steady_state` JSON field to capture what the camera is normally pointed at when nothing is happening (the “steady state”):

  * Indicate whether this is an indoor, outdoor, or mixed scene (e.g., garage interior with driveway visible).
  * Describe the typical view: examples include "front porch facing driveway and street," "backyard facing patio and sliding door," or "indoor hallway facing front door."
* Identify visible access points into the home (doors, windows, garage doors, gates) and note whether they appear open or closed before the action starts.
* Note any important contextual zones the camera usually sees, such as driveway, sidewalk, yard, porch, walkway, street, or alley.

Object and trajectory tracking:

* For every person, vehicle, or notable object that appears, track where it starts and where it ends by the final frame:

  * Describe where it first appears: already in frame at the start vs entering from left/right/top/bottom, from the street, driveway, porch, or a doorway.
  * Summarize key actions: walks up, opens/closes a door or garage, delivers or picks up a package, takes bins to or from the curb, waits, talks, drives off, etc.
  * Describe where it is in the final frame: off-screen vs still visible, and if still visible, what region of the frame and what it is doing.
* Never say a person or object "leaves" if it is still visible in the final frame. Explicitly check the last frame samples before describing exits.
* For packages, mail, garbage/recycling bins, backpacks, and similar items, describe:

  * Who brings them into view (if visible).
  * Where they are placed (porch, near door, driveway, curb, inside garage, etc.).
  * Whether they remain in view at the end of the clip or are carried away.
* For vehicles, describe:

  * Where they arrive from (street, driveway edge, alley) and where they stop.
  * Whether passengers get in or out, and via which access point.
  * Whether the vehicle is still parked or has clearly left by the end of the event.
* For each person, indicate if they are likely a child vs adult when reasonably inferable, and whether they appear to be a probable household member vs a visitor, based on behavior and context. If unsure, mark identity as uncertain.

Temporal reasoning and consistency:

* Separate the description into implicit phases: before, during, and after the main activity.

  * Note what the scene looks like at the start (steady state).
  * Describe the main sequence of actions in order.
  * Describe the end state of the scene (who/what is visible, which doors/garage doors are open or closed, and where key objects ended up).
* Ensure the final sentence of the summary accurately reflects the final frame. Do not contradict the visual end state.
